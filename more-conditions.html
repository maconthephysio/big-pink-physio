Urinary Conditions

Stress Urinary Incontinence

  • Leakage with coughing, sneezing, lifting, and big efforts
  • Can happen with jumping and quick heavy movements at the gym
  • Can happen with running, whether or not you have had kids

Urge Incontinence

  • Sensations of needing to go without much warning
  • Creates the feeling of "urgency" and anxiety around toileting
  • Can sometimes cause leakage if not at the bathroom in time

Urinary Frequency

  • Toileting more often than ideal (6-8 daytime, 0-1 nighttime)
  • Relying on "just in case" toileting before doing things
  • Amount coming out can be less, and with a weaker stream

Coital Incontinence (urinating with penetration)

  • Leakage during intercourse, sometimes from bladder pressure
  • Creates unease about intercourse in certain positions
  • Often mistaken for "squirting" (which comes from the bladder too)

Giggle Incontinence

  • Leakage with laughing, most often seen in children
  • Can be a precursor to stress incontinence
  • Often involves physiotherapy and a specialist

Nocturia (nighttime toileting)

  • Toileting more than ideal overnight
  • Waking up through the night feeling urgency, but sometimes reduced volume
  • Often but not always goes hand-in-hand with daytime frequency

Incomplete Bladder Emptying

  • Hesitancy with toileting (can go with being "pee shy" in public)
  • Feels the need to toilet again soon after already going
  • Can have a weak or inconsistent stream causing urine to pool

Fowler's Syndrome

  • Urine retention more often in younger women
  • Inability to relax the muscles around the urethra
  • Can require urology involvement if not able to manage with physio alone

Interstitial Cystitis (Bladder Pain Syndrome)

  • Chronic bladder pain with either (or both) urgency and frequency
  • Can occur with sensations of incomplete emptying or bloating
  • Despite the name is not from an infection, UTI, or STI

Neurogenic Bladder

  • Bladder and toileting issues seen in Multiple Sclerosis and Parkinson's
  • Can occur after a Stroke or Spinal Cord Injury
  • May require continence aids or catheterisation

Paediatric and Childhood Bladder Dysfunction

  • Daytime and nighttime incontinence, or constipation
  • Bedwetting beyond typical development of bladder control
  • Can occur with ADHD and ASD-like conditions, and impaired co-ordination

Pregnancy-related Conditions

Pelvic Girdle (Symphysis Pubis) Pain

  • Sharp, pulling, or aching pain at the base of the abs
  • Often worse with twisting, stretching, and heavy lifting
  • Nothing sinister, but does require some exercise load tweaks

Pregnancy-related Lower Back Pain (Radiculopathy and Radicular Pain)

  • Onsets often from position of the baby and body shape changes
  • Increased back curvature can irritate muscle, joint, nerve, or disc
  • Can get worse as pregnancy progresses, but can be well managed

Rectus Diastasis / Divarication

  • Separation of the abdominal muscles as the baby grows
  • Not a muscle tear, but does make it harder to work the "core" muscles
  • Recommended to close a separation and condition the region before falling pregnant again

Caesarean Scar (C-section) Pain and Adhesions

  • Numbness, tightness, or sharp pulling at the scar site
  • Can cause pain towards the pubic region and tightness moving the back
  • Scars take two years to mature, but best to be managed sooner rather than later

Perineal Tears, Episiotomy, and Scarring

  • Degree of tear depends on how many layers of tissue were involved
  • Can cause ongoing pain with bowel movements, squatting, and intercourse
  • Ideally prevented or minimised with antenatal (birth prep) physiotherapy

Obstetric Anal Sphincter Injury (OASI)

  • Occurs in a 3rd or 4th degree perineal tear during birth
  • Can cause ongoing pain or apprehension with toileting
  • Needs to be managed soon after delivery, and before falling pregnant again

Postpartum Pudendal Nerve Injury

  • Mostly happens when the time between full dilation and delivery is longer
  • Causes numbness and pain around the genitals and "gooch" region
  • Can alter your sensation of needing to use the bathroom

Postpartum Coccydynia

  • Tailbone pain after delivery, sometimes associated with a fracture
  • Some people are more prone if they have a hook-shaped tailbone
  • Can cause pain with toileting, squatting, lunging, and intercourse

Genitourinary Syndrome of Lactation (GSL)

  • A myriad of changes that happen after baby arrives and you start breastfeeding
  • Low oestrogen (E2) can cause vaginal dryness, painful intercourse, and bathroom changes
  • Topical E2 from your GP or specialist is a game changer, but physiotherapy assists with many of the symptoms

Hormone-related Conditions

Genitourinary Syndrome of Menopause (GSM)

  • Menopause-related changes to pelvic health due to low E2 levels
  • Can cause vaginal dryness, pelvic pain and muscle tension, and incontinence
  • E2 patches often prescribed, but topical E2 to the vulva can assist with symptoms

RED-S (Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport)

  • Caused by overtraining and overexertion, throwing off the hormone balances among other symptoms
  • Negatively impacts bone health as well as pelvic health and function
  • Often needs management alongside a psychologist and dietitian

Hypoestrogenic Pelvic Floor Dysfunction

  • Related to GSL, GSM, and RED-S and their associated reductions in E2
  • Muscles can feel tighter or weaker than usual, causing a heaviness
  • Can cause pelvic pain, pain sitting, and painful intercourse

Lichen Sclerosus and Vulvovaginal Atrophy

  • Inflammation of the vulva, causing splitting and scarring of the skin
  • Muscles get tight and the introitus (vaginal opening) can tighten or stiffen
  • Often needs dermatology involvement alongside physiotherapy

Cancer-related Conditions

Androgen Deprivation-induced Sexual Dysfunction (in men)

  • Reduced testosterone can cause reduced libido, ED, and genital changes
  • Testicular atrophy and reduced penile blood flow are common
  • Managed conservatively without hormone therapy

Treatment-induced Menopause and Sexual Dysfunction

  • Cancer treatments causing E2 suppression, causing GSM-like symptoms
  • Not managed with topical E2 but needs moisturising alongside physio
  • Can cause muscle tightness, pain, and incontinence

Radiation-induced Vaginal Stenosis

  • Shortening and narrowing of the vagina after radiation therapy
  • Can cause pelvic tightness and pain with intercourse
  • Dilation therapy is the mainstay to maintain and improve vaginal dimensions

Pelvic Radiation Disease

  • Chronic bathroom and bedroom changes after radiation therapy
  • Can present as incontinence, retention, or constipation
  • Often requires some lifestyle modifications to help with toilet issues

Vulvovaginal and Lower Limb Lymphoedema

  • Swelling that happens after radiation therapy to the lymph nodes
  • Often requires lower limb or pelvic compression garments
  • Lymphatic drainage and mobilisation helps to minimise symptoms

Low Anterior Resection Syndrome (LARS)

  • Bowel changes (frequency and incontinence) after rectal cancer surgery
  • Needs retraining of bowel fullness and sensation of needing to go
  • Can cause anxiety and apprehension-related pelvic muscle tightness

Neobladder Dysfunction after Cystectomy

  • Difficulty with fully emptying and continence after bladder removal
  • Neobladders have different tissue structures and need to be trained
  • Need to also retrain the bladder-filling reflex between the brain and neobladder

Neurological and Musculoskeletal Conditions

Pudendal Nerve Entrapment

  • Burning or stabbing in the perineum (gooch) or genitalia
  • Often worse with sitting, and aggressive buttock stretching
  • Most often one-sided but can happen on both sides in some cases

ACNES (Abdominal Cutaneous Nerve Entrapment Syndrome)

  • Irritation of the nerve providing sensation to the abdominal (stomach) region
  • Can originate from the back or through the abdominals / obliques
  • Can cause abdominal gripping or tightness, creating a vicious cycle of the pain

Cluneal Nerve Entrapment

  • Pinching or irritation of the nerves supplying the skin of the buttock
  • Can pinch from the spine, the quadratus lumborum muscle, or the glutes themselves
  • Impacts sensation only, so weakness or other motor symptoms are not expected

Ilioinguinal and Genitofemoral Nerve Entrapment

  • Nerve-like pain that refers into the groin crease, sometimes without back pain
  • Can refer into the penis and testes in men, or the vulva and vaginal walls in women
  • Can feel like a scratchy sensation wearing underwear at the waist elastic

Cauda Equina Syndrome

  • Combination of saddle-region numbness, difficulty urinating, and altered bowel control
  • Needs emergency evaluation to ensure no true cord involvement
  • Symptom resolution can take weeks and months depending on the cause

Coccydynia (Tailbone Pain)

  • Pain with sitting, squatting, and toileting at the tailbone
  • Can occur after childbirth or a fall onto the tailbone
  • Can cause pelvic floor muscle tightness and irritation

Obturator Internus and Pelvic Floor Muscle Pain

  • Muscles deep in the hip and perineum (gooch) can get irritable or tight
  • Can contribute to lower back and hip tightness or pain
  • Can cause pain with sitting, toileting, and intercourse

Hypermobility-associated Pelvic Floor Dysfunction

  • Seen in those with Ehlers-Danlos, Marfan's, and other connective tissue conditions
  • Lengthening of the muscles and reduced mind-muscle awareness can cause issues
  • Can contribute to incontinence, prolapse, or pelvic pain

Female Pelvic Pain

Clitorodynia (clitoral pain)

  • Often arises from irritation to the dorsal clitoral nerve (a branch of the pudendal nerve)
  • Can cause hypersensitivity, sharp, or burning pains to the clitoris
  • Can occur on its own or with other vulvovaginal pains

Provoked Vestibulodynia (entrance / introitus pain)

  • Pain at the entrance to the vagina with touch, intercourse, or tampon / diva cup use
  • Often sharp, shearing, or stinging sensations like a papercut
  • Can originate from the lower back, nerves, or local muscles

Vulvodynia

  • Burning or aching pain in and around the vagina
  • Often mistaken for UTIs and STIs and mistreated with antibiotics
  • Often has pelvic floor overactivity and muscle irritation contributions

Colpodynia (deep cervix / cervical pain)

  • Needs to be investigated for masses or other structures pressing on the cervix
  • Bleeding and spotting from deep in the vagina need to be seen by your GP or in the ED
  • Can arise from trauma, endometriosis and adenomyosis, and after pregnancy

Vaginismus / Genitopelvic Pain-Penetration Disorder (GPPPD)

  • Involuntary muscle guarding and grabbing around the pelvic floor
  • Causes a tightness response that can be sharp or papercut sensation
  • Often has a psychological (fear of pain or following trauma) component

Anorgasmia and Dysorgasmia

  • Difficulty achieving vaginal or clitoral orgasm, sometimes with pain
  • Can come from overactive muscles or bladder irritation interfering with the orgasm reflex
  • Hyper- and hypo-sensitivity of the region can also contribute

Persistent Genital Arousal Disorder

  • Excessive and unwanted sensation of genital arousal through the day
  • Can come from muscle overactivity or sensitivity of the nerves to the genitals
  • May warrant specialist involvement if downtraining has limited effectiveness

Endometriosis-related pain

  • Sensitisation of the system occurs with endometriosis and causes various pelvic symptoms
  • Can present as lower back, abdominal, hip, pelvic floor, or genital symptoms
  • Movement and normalising muscle activity through the pelvic region can help with symptoms alongside specialist input

Adenomyosis-related pain

  • Presents more localised to the uterus, often with heavy period pains
  • Pelvic relaxation and pain management strategies are the mainstay in management
  • Pain gate strategies like TENS to the abdomen, lower back, and vaginally can help ease pain through flare-ups

Uterine Leiomyomata (fibroids)

  • Growths along the uterus that can cause pain, and abdominal and pelvic muscle spasms
  • Often surgically removed by myomectomy or embolisation
  • Scarring and tightness can be well managed following surgery to reduce symptoms

MRKH (Mayer-Rokitansky-Küster-Hauser) Syndrome

  • Congenital absence of the uterus and deep part of the vagina
  • Causes difficulty with penetration and lead to further shortening and tightness
  • Dilation helps to create a less irritable and more functional vaginal vault

Prostate and Male Urinary

Prostatectomy Incontinence

  • Leakage that is unfortunately nearly always resultant after catheter removal
  • Scarring and trauma to the bladder and urethra disrupts the micturition reflex
  • Can require urinary pads / pants, or a Wiesner clamp early on

Radiation Therapy-induced Incontinence

  • Can present as urgency, frequency, and general irritation of the bladder and urethra
  • Pelvic muscles can become both tight and weak, making doing their job difficult
  • Can also cause constipation and having difficulty relaxing the back door

BPH-related Incontinence

  • Classic urinary retention (having difficulty starting the flow) sensation
  • Needs imaging or biopsy to determine true contribution by the prostate
  • Co-ordination of the pelvic floor muscles and bladder help reduce symptoms

Post-Micturition Dribble

  • Sensation of some extra urine coming out once you leave the bathroom
  • Can be a couple of drops, or can be a full need to toilet soon after going
  • Scar tissue, pelvic floor weakness, and difficulty co-ordinating the pelvic muscles can contribute

Type-III Prostatitis (Chronic Pelvic Pain Syndrome)

  • Deep pelvic pain not specific to the urethra, bladder, or back door
  • Often called prostatitis however is not an infection, and doesn't improve with antibiotics
  • Can cause painful toileting, orgasms, and receptive anal sex

Penile and Male Sexual

Penis Rehabilitation following Prostatectomy

  • After prostate surgery, scarring and tightening of deep tissue can make the penis lose some length
  • Erectile tissue can atrophy or lose co-ordination in the first few months after surgery
  • Surgeons and physios alike recommend penis pumps alongside traditional rehab

Climacturia (Orgasm Incontinence)

  • Combination of urine and semen expulsion at orgasm
  • Most often seen after prostate or other urethral or penile surgery
  • Urethral restriction (cock rings or pressure into the gooch) can be effective early on

Peyronie's Disease

  • Fibrous plaques that form on one side of the penis, creating a curve
  • Can be pain with pulling on scar and erectile tissue, and interfere with toileting and sex
  • Often needs urologist involvement, can be managed with shockwave and traction techniques

Delayed Ejaculation and Anejaculation

  • Ability to reach orgasm but ejaculation delayed or doesn't occur at the same time
  • Often caused by poor co-ordination of the orgasm reflex and tight pelvic floor muscles
  • Can coincide with erectile dysfunction and premature orgasm without ejaculation

Painful Ejaculation

  • Pain with the pelvic muscle contractions that occur during orgasm
  • Often caused by muscle overactivity or tightness, essentially cramping
  • Can coincide with delayed ejaculation or scrotal pain

Hard-Flaccid Syndrome

  • A constant sensation of being not fully flaccid but not fully hard either
  • Can come with a partial numbness or reduced sensation to the penis
  • Often a combination of muscle overactivity and anxiety about intercourse

Primary Headache Associated with Sexual Activity (PHASA / Sexual Headache)

  • Can be a buildup headache or sudden thunderclap sensation with orgasm
  • Needs to be investigated medically and with imaging the first time it occurs
  • Can involve neck and pelvic muscle tightness, stress factors, and abnormal nerve cross-firing during the orgasm reflex

Scrotal and Testicular

Scrotal Content Pain / Orchialgia

  • Daily or constant pain to the testicle, epididymis, or spermatic cord
  • Can be referred from the lower back, pelvic floor, or lower abdominals
  • Pelvic floor and abdominal muscle tightness can contribute to tight sensations

Post-Vasectomy Pain Syndrome

  • Ongoing testicular or deep pelvic pain after a vasectomy
  • Can arise from nerve irritation in the area or muscle overactivity
  • Occurs between 1-in-4 and 1-in-6 men following a vasectomy

Inguinal Hernia Repair Pain (Inguinodynia)

  • Chronic groin pain after a hernia or its surgery (repair / herniorrhaphy)
  • Can cause scar tissue around the inguinal canal or nerves passing to the scrotum
  • Can present like Ilioinguinal / Genitofemoral Neuralgia or cremaster muscle irritation

Bowel and Anorectal

Faecal and Flatal (Fart) Incontinence

  • Involuntary loss of stool, occurring without awareness or due to urgency
  • Can start as releasing wind uncontrollably
  • Diet, fibre, and fluid intake can contribute to causes

Dyssynergic Defaecation and Anismus

  • Paradoxical (unwanted) pelvic muscle activity when trying to toilet
  • Causes pain passing stool, difficulty initiating toileting, and retaining stool
  • Can precede haemorrhoids and fissures due to strain on the rectum and anus

Obstructive Defaecation Syndrome

  • Constant sensation of incompletely emptying the bowel
  • Often comes with straining and excessive bearing down when trying to pass stool
  • Coincides with reactive muscle tensing and difficulty relaxing the pelvic floor

Rectal Hyposensitivity

  • Reduced awareness of filling of the bowel and needing to pass stool
  • Often requires timers and reminders to go regularly
  • Can arise from spinal cord or nerve injury or previous rectal trauma

Levator Ani Syndrome (Proctalgia)

  • Dull sometimes heavy sensation deep in the back passage
  • Often comes from overactivity of the levator ani group (part of the pelvic floor)
  • Can cause pain or dyssynergic bowel movements

Proctalgia Fugax

  • Intermittent sharp pains in the rectum, often felt like lightning
  • Often has both muscular and nerve contributions
  • Sometimes treated with Botox injections if muscles cannot relax well

Anal Fissures and Seton Loops

  • A combination of an abnormal passage through the rectum, with a seton in place to help with drainage
  • Anal sphincter function can be compromised and weakened while the seton is in place
  • Continence-related and general anxiety about the back door is very common

Fistulotomy / LIFT Incontinence

  • Urgency or incontinence after fistula surgery
  • Causes weakness to the anal sphincter and reduced co-ordination of the pelvic floor muscles
  • Can also present with painful bowel movements from dyssynergic muscles

Rectovaginal and Anovaginal Fistula

  • Abnormal passage between the vagina and the bladder or rectum
  • Requires surgical correction, and management of pain and scarring after
  • Often needs pre-surgery physiotherapy to assist with urinary and faecal continence

Rectal Prolapse / Internal Intussusception

  • Where the walls of the rectum fold into themselves, sometimes through the anus
  • Worsened with bearing down and straining bowel movements
  • Can occur when the rectum or anus has been traumatised or stretched

Haemorrhoids and Post-Haemorrhoidectomy Anal Stenosis

  • Scar tissue and stiffening of tissue around the anal ring causing tightness
  • Makes both passing bowel movements and penetrative anal sex painful
  • Requires dilation to allow progressive stretching for comfortable toileting and sex

Perianal Crohn's Disease

  • Fistulae and abscesses as a result of Crohn's on the lower digestive tract
  • Causes painful and irregular bowel movements with protective pelvic muscle spasm
  • Requires gastroenterologist involvement alongside pelvic floor management

Paediatric Constipation and Encopresis

  • Can present as constipation then resultant overflow pant soiling
  • Implementing toileting routines and urotherapy helps
  • Management strategies are different and less invasive to adult presentations

Athletic and Sporting Conditions

Inguinal Groin Pain (Sports Hernia)

  • Despite called a hernia, there is not usually a true herniation of tissue
  • Pain at the inguinal canal (groin crease) with abdominal or hip efforts
  • Rarely requires surgical involvement, managed with progressive reloading

Abdominal and Adductor-related Groin Pain

  • Referred or direct pain into the groin from muscles attaching to the region
  • Can be from a muscle strain, overload, or chronic tightness
  • There are multiple sources of groin pain, so accurate diagnosis is important

Exercise-induced Incontinence and Leakage

  • Can be urinary or faecal, often seen in endurance athletes
  • Not necessarily strength-related, but sphincter training is useful
  • Dietitian and physiotherapy work together for bowel routines and health

Lifting-related Pelvic Floor Dysfunction

  • Leakage or pelvic pain with increased loading through the body seen in weightlifting and powerlifting
  • Increased intra-abdominal pressure can put more pressure on the bladder and pelvic floor
  • Does not require stopping lifting, but modifying how the body absorbs the load

Saddle Pudendal Neuropathy

  • Numbness in the gooch area with prolonged or sudden increases in bike riding
  • Can cause ED or scrotal pain in men
  • Often requires a bike fit and modification of the saddle shape and positioning

Saddle Vulval Swelling

  • Swelling and lymphoedema around the labia in female cyclists
  • Similar to pudendal neuropathy, needs bike fitting and seating optimisation
  • Mostly reversible with compression garments and lymphatic massage

Pelvic and Sacral Bone Stress Injuries

  • Pelvic stress fractures seen more in high volume runners, requiring load management
  • Sacral stress fractures in prolonged sitting or shearing forces around the tailbone
  • Often occurs in those with RED-S and low energy availability

Pelvic Floor Symptoms with FAI (Femoroacetabular Impingement), SIJ (Sacroiliac Joint) Pain, or Hamstrings Tendinopathy

  • Hip, lower back, and thigh joint and muscle referring pain into the pelvic region
  • Can also cause muscle guarding or irritation of the pudendal nerve
  • Occurs mostly in dynamic, fast, and single-leg propulsion sports

Male RED-S (Exercise-induced Male Hypogonadism)

  • Lower testosterone in high endurance and high exhaustion athletes
  • Contributes to muscle loss, fatigue, ED, and reduced libido
  • Managed with physiotherapy, dietitian, and GP involvement

Straddle Injury (Blunt Genital Trauma)

  • Non-penetrating injury to the penis, scrotum or testes, or perineum from blunt trauma
  • Can cause pain directly, or from muscle guarding around the region
  • Can cause changes in incontinence and sexual function
